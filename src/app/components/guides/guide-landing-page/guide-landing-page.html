Training Pokémon: A Trainer’s Guide

Becoming a skilled Pokémon Trainer isn't just about catching powerful creatures — it's about building strong bonds, understanding strengths and weaknesses, and training smart. Whether you're preparing for your first Gym Challenge or heading into the Pokémon League, proper training can make the difference between victory and defeat.

Experience & Leveling Up

Pokémon gain experience (EXP) points through battles. When enough EXP is accumulated, the Pokémon levels up, increasing its stats and potentially learning new moves or evolving.

  • Wild Pokémon give less EXP than Trainers or Gym Leaders.
  • EXP Share distributes EXP to all party members.
  • Some Pokémon evolve only after reaching certain levels (e.g., Charmeleon → Charizard at Lv.36).

Effort Values (EVs)

EVs are hidden points gained by defeating Pokémon in battle, which enhance specific stats. For example:

  • Defeating a Pikachu grants Speed EVs.
  • Defeating a Machop gives Attack EVs.

EV training allows competitive trainers to fine-tune their Pokémon for specific roles like tanking or sweeping.

Individual Values (IVs)

IVs are the Pokémon's genetic potential, randomly determined at capture or hatching. They affect stats and range from 0–31.

  • Use the Judge function in your PC to see IV summaries.
  • Breeding with Destiny Knot and IV items can pass IVs to offspring.

Items & Training Tools

  • Exp. Share – Distributes EXP to all party Pokémon.
  • Power Items – Help with targeted EV training.
  • Vitamins – Boost EVs instantly (Protein, Carbos, etc.).
  • Nature Mints – Adjust a Pokémon's stat growth bias.

Pro Training Tips

  • Maximize EVs (510 total, 252 per stat) for competitive Pokémon.
  • Choose Natures that complement your strategy (e.g., Jolly for speed).
  • Use S.O.S. chains, hordes, or mass outbreaks for faster training in some games.
  • Happiness-based evolutions (like Eevee to Espeon) need care, items, and walking.

Did You Know?

  • A Level 1 Pokémon with perfect IVs and EVs can defeat a Level 100 if the strategy is right.
  • Pokémon with the ability Pokérus gain double EVs — and it's contagious!
  • Some moves (like Return) grow stronger with higher friendship.

Breeding & Egg Groups

Breeding is a core mechanic that allows Trainers to produce new Pokémon with inherited moves, Abilities, and even optimized stats. Not all Pokémon can breed, and compatibility is determined by Egg Groups — a classification based on physical traits or biological compatibility.

What Are Egg Groups?

Every Pokémon that is capable of breeding belongs to one or two Egg Groups. Pokémon in the same Egg Group can produce offspring when placed together in a nursery or daycare. For example:

  • Gyarados and Milotic can breed — both are in the Water 2 group.
  • Pikachu and Skitty cannot — they share no Egg Group.

Some Egg Groups reflect body shape or function:

  • Field – Common land Pokémon (e.g., Eevee, Growlithe)
  • Water 1 / 2 / 3 – Different aquatic Pokémon types
  • Flying – Pokémon with wings or flight traits
  • Monster – Dinosaur-like or reptilian Pokémon
  • Human-Like – Humanoid species like Machop
  • Amorphous – Ghosts or blob-like creatures like Grimer

Others are unique:

  • Ditto – A special group that only includes Ditto
  • Undiscovered – Legendary/Mythical Pokémon that cannot breed

Breeding Rules

  • Breeding requires one male and one female of the same Egg Group.
  • The hatched Pokémon is always the species of the female, unless using Ditto.
  • Everstone allows passing down of Nature.
  • Moves, Abilities, Poké Balls may be inherited depending on conditions.
  • Egg Moves are special techniques only available through breeding.

Ditto – The Ultimate Breeder

Ditto belongs to its own group and can breed with almost any Pokémon that can breed, regardless of gender. It is especially valuable for:

  • Breeding genderless Pokémon
  • Producing offspring with specific Natures or IVs
  • Mass-breeding for competitive Pokémon

Breeding & Natures

When breeding, the offspring’s Nature is typically inherited from one of the parents. To pass down a desired Nature, breeders often give the parent Pokémon a Everstone to hold, which guarantees the Nature is passed on to the child.

Breeding & IVs

Regarding IVs (Individual Values), these are partially inherited from the parents. By breeding with parents that have high IVs, especially when using items like the Destiny Knot, it is possible to pass down multiple strong IV stats to the offspring, allowing breeders to produce Pokémon with optimized stats for battle.

Additionally, certain Power Items can be held by the parent Pokémon to help pass down specific IV stats to their offspring. These include:

  • Power Weight (HP)
  • Power Bracer (Attack)
  • Power Belt (Defense)
  • Power Lens (Special Attack)
  • Power Band (Special Defense)
  • Power Anklet (Speed)

When a parent holds one of these Power Items, the IV stat corresponding to that item is guaranteed to be passed down to the offspring, making them invaluable tools for targeted IV breeding.

Understanding EVs and IVs in Pokémon

In the world of Pokémon training, two crucial hidden mechanics influence a Pokémon’s strength and potential: Effort Values (EVs) and Individual Values (IVs). These stats determine how strong a Pokémon can become at its maximum level, making them essential for competitive battling and team building.

What are IVs?

Individual Values (IVs) are like a Pokémon's genes. They are fixed when you first encounter or hatch a Pokémon and cannot be changed (except with Hyper Training). IVs determine how good a Pokémon can become in each stat — HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense, and Speed.

  • Each stat has an IV ranging from 0 to 31.
  • A stat with a 31 IV is considered “perfect.”
  • IVs are randomly generated when a Pokémon is caught or bred.
  • To check IVs in-game, unlock the Judge function in your PC boxes.

What are EVs?

Effort Values (EVs) are points gained through battling or consuming specific items. Unlike IVs, EVs can be trained and customized to improve specific stats based on how you raise your Pokémon.

  • You can earn EVs by defeating specific Pokémon that grant them.
  • Each Pokémon can gain a total of 510 EVs across all stats.
  • Up to 252 EVs can be applied to a single stat.
  • Every 4 EVs in a stat equals +1 actual stat point at level 100.

How EVs and IVs Impact Stats

A Pokémon’s final stats at level 100 are determined by a combination of:

  1. Base Stats (species-specific)
  2. IVs (innate potential)
  3. EVs (training effort)
  4. Nature (stat modifier)
  5. Level

For example, a Pokémon with a high Speed base stat, a 31 IV in Speed, and 252 EVs in Speed will be much faster than average. Competitive players carefully optimize EVs to boost the most useful stats for a specific strategy.

Tips for Training EVs

  • Use items like Vitamins (e.g., Carbos, Protein) to quickly boost EVs.
  • Battle specific wild Pokémon for targeted EV gains — e.g., Zubat gives Speed EVs.
  • Equip Power Items (e.g., Power Anklet) to gain more EVs per battle.
  • Use berries like Pomeg or Kelpsy to remove unwanted EVs and reallocate them.

Hyper Training and Bottle Caps

Since IVs are unchangeable by default, Hyper Training allows you to simulate perfect IVs using Bottle Caps. This makes your Pokémon viable in competitive play without requiring perfect breeding.

Summary

EVs and IVs are fundamental to building strong Pokémon. IVs determine a Pokémon's natural potential, while EVs allow you to shape its strengths during training. Mastering both systems is key to success in battles — whether in competitive formats or high-level in-game challenges.

Pokémon Natures

Natures play a vital role in shaping a Pokémon’s stats and personality. While they might seem like flavor text at first glance, each nature subtly adjusts how a Pokémon grows — raising one stat by 10% and lowering another by 10%. This difference becomes most noticeable at higher levels, especially level 100.

There are 25 unique Natures in total. Of these, 20 Natures influence stats, while 5 Natures are considered neutral — they raise and lower the same stat, effectively canceling out any impact.

Choosing the right Nature can optimize a Pokémon’s performance in battle. For instance, a Pokémon that primarily uses physical attacks may benefit from an Adamant Nature (↑Attack, ↓Special Attack), while a fast special attacker might prefer Timid (↑Speed, ↓Attack).

Stat Modifiers by Nature

  • Attack: Raised by Lonely, Adamant, Naughty, Brave
  • Defense: Raised by Bold, Impish, Lax, Relaxed
  • Special Attack: Raised by Modest, Mild, Rash, Quiet
  • Special Defense: Raised by Calm, Gentle, Careful, Sassy
  • Speed: Raised by Timid, Hasty, Jolly, Naive

Neutral Natures

Hardy, Docile, Serious, Bashful, and Quirky are considered neutral — they don’t alter a Pokémon's stats in any way and are typically used when no specific optimization is needed.

Berry Preferences

Natures also determine a Pokémon’s taste in Berries. Each stat corresponds to a flavor:

  • Spicy → Attack
  • Sour → Defense
  • Dry → Special Attack
  • Bitter → Special Defense
  • Sweet → Speed

A Pokémon will enjoy the flavor linked to its raised stat and dislike the flavor tied to its lowered stat. For example, a Pokémon with a Modest Nature (↑Sp. Atk, ↓Attack) will like Dry Berries and dislike Spicy ones.

Understanding and utilizing Natures allows Trainers to fine-tune their Pokémon for battle, breeding, and contests — making them an essential detail in raising competitive teams.

Pokémon Battle Styles

Casual Battles

Casual battles are friendly matches between trainers that often focus more on creativity, favorite Pokémon, or theme teams rather than strict competition. These battles may ignore EV training or optimized strategies, allowing players to enjoy the thrill of battle without the pressure of meta or tier lists.

Competitive Singles

In competitive single battles (1v1), strategy is key. Players focus on building balanced teams of six, using tools like EV/IV training, nature optimization, type synergy, and prediction. Popular formats like Smogon tiers help define rulesets, such as OU (OverUsed), UU (UnderUsed), and Ubers.

Competitive Doubles

Double battles involve two Pokémon on the field per side and are the default format for official tournaments. Doubles emphasize coordination, synergy, and speed control, with moves like Protect, Follow Me, and Helping Hand playing critical roles. Weather and terrain effects are more impactful in this format.

Official VGC Format

The Video Game Championships (VGC) is the official competitive format endorsed by The Pokémon Company. VGC battles are 4v4 double battles, selected from a team of six. Rules are updated annually, including bans on certain legendary Pokémon or items, and usually only allow Pokémon available in the current generation or regional Pokédex.

VGC focuses on fast-paced, strategic play and rewards adaptability, prediction, and understanding of the evolving meta. Popular strategies include Trick Room teams, Intimidate cycling, and Tera-type optimization in recent generations.

Other Popular Formats

Aside from standard singles and doubles, many players enjoy formats like Monotype (all team members share a type), Battle Stadium, Little Cup (only unevolved Pokémon), and Draft League (players draft unique teams and compete over weeks). These formats allow for unique strategies and often encourage creative team building.

Unique Battle Styles in Spin-Off Games

Pokémon Legends: Arceus

In Legends: Arceus, battles blend real-time and turn-based mechanics, breaking traditional norms. Players can approach wild Pokémon stealthily or engage directly. The battle system introduces two unique battle styles that influence move order and effects:

  • Agile Style: Moves used in Agile Style allow the Pokémon to act faster, increasing the chance to move first in battle. Agile moves often have reduced power but can be crucial for quick strategic plays, such as setting up status or hitting first.
  • Strong Style: Moves used in Strong Style sacrifice priority and speed for increased power, delivering a harder-hitting attack that often has additional effects like breaking through shields or causing recoil.

This system encourages tactical choices between speed and power on each turn, adding fresh depth to Pokémon battles in the Hisui region.

Pokémon Ranger Series

Pokémon Ranger introduces a unique capture mechanic using a "capture styler" device, where players draw circles around Pokémon on the touchscreen to catch them temporarily. Battles are more action oriented and less about strategy, focusing on quick reflexes and mini-game style challenges.

Pokémon XD: Gale of Darkness & Colosseum

Both Pokémon XD: Gale of Darkness and Pokémon Colosseum for the GameCube feature 3D battle arenas with double battles as the core format. These games introduce the concept of "Shadow Pokémon," which require purification, adding a story-driven element to battles. The style combines classic turn-based strategy with unique battle animations and story mechanics.

Pokémon Mystery Dungeon

In the Pokémon Mystery Dungeon series, battles happen in turn-based dungeon crawls where positioning on a grid matters. The style focuses on exploration and survival against waves of wild Pokémon, blending RPG and roguelike elements.
